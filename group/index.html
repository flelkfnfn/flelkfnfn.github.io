    How the guess works

    Every year, the U.S. Social Security Administration counts how many babies got each first name. For every name you type, we look up its share of births in each year from 1925 to 2025. Jennifer, for example, took off in the 1960s and peaked in 1972.

    Then we score every year by how common all of your names were at the same time. Years where every name was popular score highest; one name that barely existed that year pulls the score way down. The top year is our guess for when your group was born, and the range covers 80% of the likelihood.

    It works best with four or more names of people born within a few years of each other. Mixing parents and kids widens the range. Common nicknames are read as the full name (Jen counts as Jennifer, Mike as Michael), and only the first word of a full name is used. Spelling counts, too: Caitlin peaked in 1988, Kaitlyn in 2000.
